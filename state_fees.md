## Fee Definitions

- **Registration**: Annual base registration fee paid to the state DMV
- **Title**: One-time fee to title the vehicle in owner's name
- **Plates**: Fee for standard license plates (if separate from registration)
- **EV Surcharge**: Additional annual fee for electric/plug-in hybrid vehicles (to offset lost gas tax revenue)
- **Doc Fee Cap**: Maximum dealer documentation/processing fee allowed by state law ("No cap" = state does not limit)
- **Notes**: Key state-specific details affecting total cost

## Important Caveats

1. **Fees change frequently.** These are approximate 2024-2025 values and should be verified with each state's DMV.
2. **County/municipal fees** can add $5-$100+ on top of state fees (especially in CO, GA, NC, VA).
3. **Weight-based fees** are shown as ranges; the actual amount depends on vehicle GVWR.
4. **Age-based fees** decrease as the vehicle ages in many states (CO, ID, ND, etc.).
5. **Sales/use tax** is separate from these fees and is typically calculated on the purchase price.
6. **Trade-in credit**: Most states allow sales tax credit for trade-in value, but not all (CA, DC, HI, KY, MD, MI, VA, WV do NOT).
7. **"Included"** under Plates means the plate fee is bundled into the registration fee.
8. **Doc fee caps** represent the legal maximum; actual dealer doc fees may be lower.
9. **EV surcharges** typically apply to battery-electric vehicles; plug-in hybrids may pay a reduced rate (often 50-70% of the BEV fee).

## States With No Sales Tax on Vehicles

- Alaska (AK) - no state sales tax (local taxes may apply)
- Delaware (DE) - no sales tax (but has 4.25% document fee on purchase)
